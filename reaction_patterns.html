General Reaction Patterns

Master these "Master Formulas" to predict any product in the exam.

1. Acid + Base (Neutralization)

Acid + Base → Salt + Water
  • Example: HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O
  • Key: H⁺ ions from acid and OH⁻ ions from base combine to form H₂O.

2. Acid + Metal

Acid + Metal → Salt + Hydrogen Gas (H₂)
  • Example: Zn + H₂SO₄ → ZnSO₄ + H₂
  • Observation: Brisk effervescence; gas burns with a 'pop' sound.

3. Metal Carbonate + Acid

Metal Carbonate + Acid → Salt + CO₂ + H₂O
  • Example: Na₂CO₃ + 2HCl → 2NaCl + H₂O + CO₂
  • Test: CO₂ turns lime water milky.

4. Metal Oxide + Water

Metal Oxide + Water → Metal Hydroxide (Base)
  • Example: CaO (Quick lime) + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ (Slaked lime)
  • Nature: Metal oxides are generally basic.

Board Exam "Reasoning" Questions

Bulleted answers for high-weightage conceptual questions.

Q: Why should curd/sour substances not be kept in brass or copper vessels?

  • Curd and sour substances contain acids.
  • Acids react with metals (copper/brass) to form toxic metallic salts.
  • These salts can cause food poisoning and make the food unfit for consumption.

Q: Why does an aqueous solution of an acid conduct electricity?

  • Conduction of electricity requires the movement of free ions.
  • In water, acids undergo dissociation to produce H⁺ (aq) or H₃O⁺ ions.
  • These mobile ions carry electric charge through the solution.

Q: Why do ionic compounds have high melting points?

  • Ionic compounds consist of positive and negative ions held by strong electrostatic forces.
  • A large amount of thermal energy is required to break these strong inter-ionic attractions.
  • Hence, they remain solid at room temperature and melt only at very high temperatures.

Important Compounds & Their Uses

1. Bleaching Powder CaOCl₂

  • Uses:
    • Bleaching cotton and linen in textile industries.
    • Disinfecting drinking water to make it germ-free.
    • As an oxidizing agent in chemical industries.

2. Baking Soda NaHCO₃

  • Chemical Name: Sodium Hydrogen Carbonate
  • Uses:
    • Making baking powder (Baking soda + Tartaric acid).
    • As an Antacid to neutralize excess stomach acid.
    • Used in soda-acid fire extinguishers.

3. Plaster of Paris CaSO₄.½H₂O

  • Formation: Heating Gypsum at 373 K.
  • Uses:
    • Supporting fractured bones in the right position.
    • Making toys, decorative materials, and smooth surfaces for walls.
